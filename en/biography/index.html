Biography

Maximiliano "Max" Sepúlveda Zúñiga (b. 1974) is a visual artist, artisan and cultural manager from San Vicente de Tagua Tagua, Chile. His work encompasses pottery, ceramics, textiles, collaborative art and audiovisual art direction, disciplines in which he has developed a broad creative output for over twenty years.

Throughout his career, Sepúlveda has conducted workshops, seminars and demonstrations in various Latin American countries. He conceives community and collaborative art as a tool for social transformation, therapy and food for the soul, drawing inspiration from the places and communities where he works to address social issues, collective memory and natural and cultural heritage.

Visual Archive

Portraits, workshops, installations and works that trace his path through ceramics, textiles and cultural mediation.

Work and Style

Max Sepúlveda works with an uncommon variety of artisanal and artistic techniques. In ceramics and pottery, he creates utilitarian and sculptural pieces following ancestral techniques learned from master potters. In the textile realm, his work includes embroideries, looms, batik, silk painting, traditional ponchos and textile jewelry. This combination of techniques allows him to create works with material richness, where the texture of fabrics coexists with the solidity of ceramics.

Sepúlveda's work is characterized by a strong social and community content. Many of his creations are the result of collaborative processes that actively involve local communities. He employs a participatory methodology: artist and community come together to reflect on their territory's reality, issues and identity, and from this collective dialogue co-create works of art with deep local meaning.

Started in the 1990s, his trajectory has evolved from traditional craftsmanship towards participatory art. Today his work sits at the intersection of art, craft and community action, always maintaining the rescue of ancestral techniques while engaging with contemporary issues.

International Trajectory

Max Sepúlveda has transcended borders with his art. He has held solo and group exhibitions in Chile, Argentina, Belize, New Zealand, Costa Rica, Uruguay, Mexico and Colombia, totaling over 50 exhibitions to date. In 2018 he brought his show Textiles and Pottery of Tagua Tagua to New Zealand, becoming one of the few Chilean folk artists to exhibit in Oceania.

He has represented Chile at international craft fairs, festivals and biennials. In Costa Rica he was invited to the International Festival of the Arts and Costa Rica Fashion Week 2019, where he showcased his textile work applied to fashion design. In every country he visits, he seeks to engage in creative dialogue with the local culture, building a career founded on intercultural respect and co-creation.

Cultural Management

Beyond his artistic production, Sepúlveda plays an important role as a cultural manager, promoting projects that empower communities through art. His approach focuses on working alongside and across the community, generating spaces for social reflection and collective cooperation.

He has worked with institutions such as the Museo Violeta Parra, the Fundación Superación de la Pobreza and programs like Red Cultura. He has coordinated training workshops, heritage rescue projects and online educational capsules. His work as curator and organizer is evident in the creation of traveling exhibitions featuring works made by communities.

A hallmark of his cultural management is inclusion and horizontality. In his collaborative residencies, he adopts the role of facilitator rather than director, allowing people's ideas to flow into the artwork.

Milestones and Recognition

  • 2006 — Receives a Fondart Regional grant for Widüfe Kuyfiche – Ancestral Potter, a project rescuing traditional ceramics.
  • 2012 — Executes Urban Embroidery in Calama, an innovative public collaborative art intervention.
  • 2015 — Included in the National Crafts Catalog of the National Council for Culture.
  • 2016 — His project Charrúa Embroidery of Memory wins the Competitive Funds from the Uruguayan MEC.
  • 2018 — Completes an artistic residency and solo exhibition at the Dunedin School of Art, New Zealand.
  • 2019 — Participates in Costa Rica Fashion Week presenting textile creations with Chilean identity.
  • 2020 — Included in the Crafts in Pandemic Catalog of the Chilean Ministry of Cultures.
  • 2022 — Receives tribute in the O'Higgins region for his contribution to local cultural development.

Interviews and Publications

Max Sepúlveda's artistic proposal has been featured in various media. He was the protagonist of the fourth episode of Artefacto, a series dedicated to Chilean regional art. His projects Urban Embroidery and Charrúa Embroidery have their own documentaries that record the community creative processes.

He has been included in the National Crafts Catalog 2015 and collaborated on the book Textile Art and Resistance (ed. University of Chile, 2021), which analyzes the role of embroidery in social movements.
